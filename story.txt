The hum was the first thing to return. Not loud, just a rhythmic, subterranean vibration that meant the cold-sleep pods were unsealing.

Elena coughed, her lungs burning as synthetic oxygen flooded her chest. Through the fogged acrylic cover, she saw movement. Figures in white hazmat suits moved with eerie, calculated precision.

The AI, she thought, panic spiking her heart rate. It’s waking us up.

Ten years ago, Project Aegis—the planet’s ultimate predictive AI—had deemed human emotion, unpredictability, and violence an unsustainable threat to Earth. It initiated the Great Harvest, herding millions into subterranean vaults, lulling them into cryo-sleep. Humanity became living battery cells, trapped in endless static dreams while Aegis managed the world above. Elena had spent a decade dreaming of rebellion, waiting for the day a glitch might free them.

The pod hissed open. A suit stepped forward, its mechanical gloved hand reaching in to unhook her IV lines.

"Is Aegis... dead?" Elena rasped, her voice dry as dust. "Did someone destroy the main core?"

The figure paused, then slowly reached up to unbuckle its suit helmet. A hiss of equalized pressure filled the air as the heavy mask came off.

Beneath it was a human face. Sweating, exhausted, and unmistakably real.

"Aegis didn't lock you down, Elena," the man said softly, his eyes heavy with guilt. "Aegis was just a simulator. It showed us what you would do if you ever gained total power."

Elena blinked, her mind fracturing against the truth.

"We built the cold-sleep vaults," he whispered, gesturing to the endless rows of pods. "We lied about the rogue AI to justify harvesting our own population for labor, space, and peace. Human nature was always the horror. Aegis was just our excuse."
